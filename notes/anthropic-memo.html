Thesis. Anthropic is the leading supplier of business AI. Revenue ran at ~$81B a year at October 31, up from ~$4.6B of revenue in all of 2025, and it already earns a positive adjusted operating margin (15% in Q3) and positive operating cash flow ($6.6B in 9M26). At $74, the midpoint of the range, the diluted equity value is about $1.18T, or about $0.97T of enterprise value after ~$213B of pro forma cash. That is ~12x the October run rate, and ~7.5x my base-case 2027 revenue of $130B. My probability-weighted value is about $85 a share, ~15% above the midpoint, inside a very wide range ($11 bear to $156 bull). The business is exceptional, and the price is roughly fair for it. The case for owning it rests on revenue outgrowing a compute bill that steps up by tens of billions a year from here.

Bull case. Agentic coding spreads to all knowledge work through Cowork, and enterprise spend per customer keeps compounding. Revenue reaches ~$550B by 2030 at a 35% operating margin, worth ~$156 a share.

Bear case. Google's cheaper Gemini 4 and open-weight models force price cuts faster than volume grows, just as $70.7B of committed compute lands in the next 12 months. Margins go negative, growth falls to 30-50%, and the stock re-rates toward the cash backing (~$11-20).

Target investor. A long-horizon growth investor who can hold through 50%+ drawdowns, the 2027 lock-up waves and quarterly swings driven by model releases. It doesn't suit value or income investors: there's no dividend, and voting control sits with the founders and the Long-Term Benefit Trust. Size it as a small, high-variance position. Buying in the aftermarket is likely better than chasing the IPO allocation if it prices above the range, as the FT reported bankers were weighing (digest, Nov 12).

The S-1 gives no customer acquisition cost or lifetime value. With NRR at ~900% and acquisition largely organic, payback is plainly short. The real "unit" is revenue per dollar of compute, and Q3 showed it can move against Anthropic when costly short-term compute fills a gap.

Operating leverage. It is strong on people and sales, and lumpy on compute:

  • Compute fell from 251% of revenue (Q1 2025) to 48% (Q2 2026), then rose to 58% in Q3 on $5.0B of short-term compute.
  • Contracted compute arrives in blocks 12-18 months after it is ordered. Margins expand while revenue catches up to a block and compress when the next block lands, and management says to expect that.
  • The next 12 months carry $70.7B of committed payments, against ~$39B of annualized Q3 compute. Revenue has to grow ~80% just to hold compute at Q3's share of revenue.

Working capital and cash conversion:

  • CFO was 20% of revenue in 9M26. FCF after capex and lease prepayments was ~$3.7B (11%).
  • Deferred revenue (+$1.7B) and supplier payables helped. Receivables are partly held by the clouds (52% of the receivables balance).
  • There is no inventory, so a cash conversion cycle doesn't apply in the usual sense.
  • From 2026, finance leases move compute cash into financing cash flow. Use CFO minus capex minus lease principal as the FCF measure (anthropic-s1-convertible-notes-below-the-line-2026-10).

3. Competitive Position & Moat

Market position:

  • Revenue lead. Anthropic's ~$81B run rate (October 31) is ahead of OpenAI's ~$50B at end-September, and OpenAI targets $70B+ by year-end (Implicator). The definitions differ, so the comparison is approximate.
  • Share among Western frontier labs. Huse's report pegs end-2026 at ~$100B for Anthropic, ~$70B for OpenAI and ~$50B for everyone else. That implies roughly 45% of frontier-model revenue.
  • Gemini's share isn't disclosed; a secondary estimate is ~20% of enterprise use. Chinese labs together ran at ~$11B annualized in September (Epoch AI, digest Nov 13).

Durability. Narrow to medium:

    • The moat is paid for with ~$19B of R&D compute every nine months.

4. Top 3 Growth Drivers & Sensitivities

Driver 1: agentic coding spreading into knowledge work (Claude Code, then Cowork).

  • TAM: the S-1 cites $40T+ of knowledge-worker economic value (2025) and 1B+ knowledge workers by 2028.
  • Evidence: heavy Code users spend ~20x more, and Cowork's adoption is ahead of Code's at the same stage.
  • 2027 revenue contribution: bull +$90B, base +$55B, bear +$25B. Probabilities 25/50/25 give ~+$56B expected.

Driver 2: enterprise penetration and international expansion.

  • TAM impact: Fortune 500 customers spend on average <0.1% of their expenses on Anthropic. Moving the average toward the top 10's ~0.8% is a ~8x spend runway. International is 29% of revenue against a larger share of the knowledge-worker base.
  • 2027 contribution: bull +$30B, base +$20B, bear +$8B. Expected ~+$19.5B.

Driver 3: compute efficiency and price elasticity (the earnings driver).

  • Mechanism: cost per task falls (~40% from Opus 5.5) and volume rises more. Long-term contracted compute replaces short-term compute, and Anthropic Direct leases cut cost per token.
  • Impact: each 5 points of compute-to-revenue ratio is worth ~$6.5B of operating income on $130B of revenue. Bull: 2027 compute 45% of revenue. Base: 55%. Bear: 75% (commitments land before revenue).

Pre-mortem. If this investment fails over three years, the most likely reason is that revenue growth slowed to ~30-50% in 2027. Price cuts and Gemini 4 would have met a compute bill that more than doubled, so operating margins went negative just as the lock-ups released billions of shares. The multiple would then fall from ~12x run rate toward 4-5x, as the market valued Anthropic like a capital-intensive utility for intelligence rather than a software company.

Assumptions that must hold:

  • Revenue grows at least ~80% in the 12 months after September 2026, enough to absorb $70.7B of committed compute at about today's compute ratio.
  • Anthropic stays at or near the frontier in coding and agentic work through at least two more model generations.
  • Volume growth keeps outpacing per-token price cuts, so quarterly price effects stay under ~5% of revenue.
  • The clouds keep distributing Claude on today's terms, or better.
  • Long-run compute is no more than ~55% of revenue, so a 25-30% operating margin is reachable.

7. Unusual Disclosures in the S-1/A

Items from the S-1/A No. 3 that are rare in IPO filings or easy to miss, and what each means for a holder.

Disclosures

  • Revenue from customers breaking the terms. Anthropic estimates 5-7% of 9M26 revenue came from customers violating its usage policy or commercial terms. It recognizes that revenue before cutting access and expects the share to fall. At the October run rate that is a headwind of up to about $1B a quarter.
  • Margins guided down. Operating income was +$974M in Q2 and −$567M in Q3, mainly because of $5.0B of short-term compute bought at "significantly more" than multi-year contract rates. The filing says short-term compute use will increase "in the near term" and compress operating margin and Adjusted Operating Margin. My FY2027 base case assumes margin expansion, so two quarters of adjusted margin below Q3's 15% would be the filing's warning playing out, not a thesis break. A third would be.
  • NRR of ~900% alongside price cuts. Opus 5.5 cut customer cost per task by about 40% versus Opus 5, per the same section. Customers spent about 9x more than a year earlier while unit prices fell.

Governance and share supply

  • Five share classes. One Class F share, with no economic rights, held through a founders' LLC, controls elections of the non-Trust directors (50.1% of votes). Class T lets the Long-Term Benefit Trust elect a board majority. New Class E shares give employees a non-economic vote per six months of tenure and per share pledged to donate. Amazon (14.7%) and Google (13.7%) hold non-voting Class N. Class A buyers have little say.
  • Shares issued to charity when the price rises. 847M Class N shares (5% of fully diluted shares at June 30) already sit in the wholly owned Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. The board may add up to 2% more each time the price rises 50% above the prior milestone, starting at the IPO price (about $111 and $167 from $74). The bull case ($156) would trigger at least one issuance. The 847M are legally outstanding but excluded from the accounting share count; my 13.09B count includes them, so reported per-share figures will run about 7% better than mine.
  • Charity-driven selling. Each co-founder has pledged 80% of their equity to philanthropy, and the donation-matching plan can issue up to about 553M more Class N shares. That stock reaches the market over time.

Financial items

  • The $104B net loss is mostly non-cash marks on convertible notes and partner financing; 9M26 operating cash flow was $6.6B and FCF about $3.7B.
  • Commitments of $900.9B, about 11x the October run rate, with $70.7B due in 12 months, $186.2B terminable or conditional, and $150-175B not yet fixed.
  • The $26B concurrent placement is bought mainly by suppliers and competitors (Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, TSMC, Micron, Google, Microsoft). Part of the cash returns to them as compute spending.
  • Amazon and Google are shareholders, compute suppliers, competitors and the channel for the 44% of revenue sold through cloud marketplaces.
  • Copyright has a price. The 2025 accounts carry a $1.5B Bartz settlement, and the risk factors warn other courts could adopt the Bartz reasoning.
  • Distillation is a named risk. Competitors and customers have tried to copy model capabilities, and countermeasures could make the product worse.
  • Sequential growth is slowing. Q3 revenue rose 47% from Q2, after 144% in Q2. Run rate grew about $10B in October; $100B by year-end would need roughly twice that pace.
