Anthropic S-1: Convertible Notes, Finance Leases and Below-the-Line Costs

After Anthropic filed its S-1 (2026-10-26, accession 0001628280-26-067168), an X thread led by @rdd147 claimed Anthropic "hid all their expenses via convertible notes redeemable at IPO, $108 billion worth", likening it to dotcom firms that paid suppliers in stock. Is any operating cost hidden or understated by putting it below the line, and what could cause that in future filings?

Short answer

  • No operating cost is hidden in the S-1 periods. Every note was sold for cash. The cash was then spent through ordinary compute, personnel and other opex. The $72.4B 1H26 loss on the notes is a below-the-line, non-cash fair-value mark from the stock price rising, and it makes reported net income worse.
  • The real risks are future ones, and they sit mainly in the cash flow statement. Finance leases (about $161B of Broadcom TPU rent) will move part of compute cost below the line as interest and move lease principal out of CFO. An undrawn $42B Broadcom facility would let Anthropic pay rent in notes, which would leave no cash flow trace. The S-1 calls the facility a backstop ("if needed"), and on its terms it would be expensive to use.

How each payment method hits the statements: compute-payment-methods-statement-impact. Two below-the-line items to understand

  1. Note marks, (72,388). The Amazon rollover notes convert at a fixed $2.09–5.05 per share, so their value tracks the share price. At the IPO they convert at the lower of the fixed price and the IPO price, up to Amazon's 12% ownership cap. Any amount over the cap is reissued as rollover notes that convert quarterly. Some notes may therefore stay outstanding after the IPO and keep producing marks. Any balance not converted by maturity is repaid in cash, or set off against amounts Amazon owes Anthropic under commercial agreements.
  2. Other income, (8,757). This is mostly a (9,405) loss on the March/April 2026 Google and Amazon note purchase agreements, which sold Series G-2 at $259.14 ($25.91 split-adjusted) for $10.0B and $5.0B, plus a +724 gain on warrant rights. The agreements were first recorded as a $2.3B asset, and the loss came as the valuation rose to Series H ($589.01, or $58.90 split-adjusted) in May. They were signed in the same months as Google's $114.5B and Amazon's $110B compute commitments. The S-1 gives no pricing linkage and no breakdown of compute spend by vendor.

Broadcom: finance leases and the $42B rent-note facility

  • Leases: five-year master equipment leases for TPU hardware. The lessees are two Anthropic special-purpose subsidiaries; Anthropic guarantees them and the leased equipment secures them, with cross-defaults. Rent is about $161.2B "excluding interest", paid quarterly from "ready for service", and is non-cancelable except on default. Accounted for as finance leases. Broadcom assigned part of one lease to a third party in Q2 2026.
    • $125.2B is the one master lease filed as an exhibit (the 2026 Lease Arrangement); $161.2B is all Broadcom rent. Reuters' "about a third" refers to $42B against the $125.2B. The other agreements (Google, Amazon, Microsoft) are not filed.[1]
    • Lessor-side financing (not in the S-1): IFRE reports that a lessor vehicle buys the chips, funded by a $61.5B loan package: $42B of senior loans in two $21B tranches (SOFR+187.5bp and SOFR+150bp, maturing 2033-09-30) partly guaranteed by Broadcom, an $18B junior tranche (Blackstone reportedly about $9B, at a 10% coupon according to The Complex Rebel) and a $1.5B term loan. This sits on the lessor side, separate from Anthropic's lessee subsidiaries and from the $42B rent-note facility. Broadcom's 5-year CDS reached 133bp.[2]
  • Status at 6/30/26: finance leases were only $445M of ROU assets and $443M of liabilities (7.1% rate, 4.9 years); amortization 13, interest 5, principal paid 4. Leases signed but not yet started totaled $321.4B of future payments.
  • Infrastructure Partner 2026 NPA: up to $42B of notes, "solely to finance quarterly rent payments", issued "if needed".
    • Cost: 9% paid in kind (13% if revenue conditions are missed); Broadcom converts at 90% (85%) of the 30-day VWAP; 150% cash on a change of control.
    • The draw period hasn't started, and no notes are expected before the IPO.
    • The S-1 says Anthropic is "not dependent on" it.
    • A similar $10B facility from August 2025 was never drawn and ends at the IPO.
  • Assessment: at these terms, with $131.7B of cash and a $15B revolver, the facility looks like insurance against a cash squeeze. A draw would itself tell you something about liquidity.

Other vendor-linked equity facilities (undrawn)

  • Google 2026 NPA: $20B left (after $10B went into Series H), through 2030. Draws are tied to compute agreements and TTM revenue targets, settled in notes or stock at the 30-day VWAP, with a 14.99% ownership cap.
  • Amazon 2026 NPA: $15B left, released in $5B increments at compute-capacity milestones, settled in stock at VWAP, with a 12% cap.
  • AMD: up to $5B of stock purchases, released at GPU deployment milestones; first expected in 2027.

Published commentary (as of 2026-10-27)

Consensus on the notes: every analysis read agrees the $72.4B is a non-cash fair-value mark that stops at conversion. None claims expenses were paid in notes. The credit reading puts it this way: no credit agreement "lets a mark on the borrower's own converts affect EBITDA", so for creditors it is not a cost and for equity holders it is dilution.[3] Artificial Diligence adds that the marks "still record a real transfer" of value to Amazon and Google.[1] Amazon booked $62.8B of gains on its Anthropic preferred stock in 1H26, the other side of these marks.[4]

Cash flow: CFO is real but helped by working capital timing. Cestrian calls it "very strong", and the credit reading puts FCF at about $2.3B without working capital.[5]

Where writers say costs are understated (forward-looking):

  • Finance leases. "The leased equipment never appears as capex," so CFO − capex will overstate the cash left after compute.[1] Moving capacity to finance leases raises EBITDA "without any economic change", and lenders should deduct finance-lease principal from FCF.[3] EBITDA point: ROU amortization stays above operating income but sits below EBITDA. Under any EBITDA-style metric, all of the Broadcom rent drops out, not just the interest.
  • RSU catch-up. The S-1 has recognized no expense on RSUs that vest on time plus the IPO, because the performance condition was not yet met. When the IPO closes it books a cumulative $2.9B, then $23.7B over about 2.1 years, plus $3.1B on other awards: $26.6B of RSU expense in total. Artificial Diligence puts the 1H26 operating margin at about −18% with it, against −4% reported.
  • Channel fees and compute allocation. Cloud-marketplace fees are about 17% of revenue and sit below gross profit; counting them as cost of revenue cuts gross margin from 85% to about 68% (Artificial Diligence). Management decides how compute is split between cost of revenue and R&D, and the functional split is not given by quarter.[5]
  • Commitments. Totals range from $518B to about $1T depending on overlap and estimated tranches, and the aggregate at 9/30 is left blank.[6] The credit reading treats take-or-pay as "debt-like" and estimates that covering it needs about $133B of revenue, roughly 2.9× the June run rate.

Broadcom framing: most coverage calls it circular financing, with Broadcom as supplier, lessor and lender at once (Reuters, Forkast, Proactive, 24/7 Wall St, ZeroHedge). No piece found works through the accounting of paying rent with notes, including that a draw leaves no trace in any cash flow section (compute-payment-methods-statement-impact method 6).
