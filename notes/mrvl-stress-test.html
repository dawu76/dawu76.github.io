Bull Case

1. Secular TAM and structural market position

  • Management sizes the CY2030 addressable market at ~$400B on ~$3T of data center capex: $235B custom, $85B switching and storage, $65B interconnect, $15B comms. That is about 13% of capex. Marvell's FY2027 guide of ~$12B is roughly 3% of it, so the company can gain share instead of needing the whole pie to grow.
  • The move from copper to optics adds a second growth vector inside each capex dollar. Scale-up optics goes from about zero to "multiple hundreds of millions" in FY2028. Celestial AI CPO targets a $500M run rate by Q4 FY2028 and $1B by Q4 FY2029. Coherent DCI roughly doubles, from ~$500M in FY2026 to >$1B in FY2028.
  • Marvell's merchant PAM4 DSP lead shows up in product timing. Its 3nm Ara 1.6T DSP is shipping in volume. Broadcom's competing Taurus BCM83640 was announced Mar 11, 2026 and is still slated for mass production in late 2026. Morningstar reports Microsoft sole-sources Marvell optical chips for its North American regional data centers.
  • Marvell is the #2 custom partner with 20+ design wins. Custom revenue more than doubles in FY2028, exceeds $12B in FY2029 and reaches ~$30B in FY2031. The Google warrant framework covers up to $120B of cumulative purchases, which locks in a multi-year relationship.

2. Revenue quality

  • Non-GAAP gross margin was 58.9% in Q2 FY2027, and the Q3 guide is 57.5-58.5%. That held even as lower-margin custom silicon grew to a larger share of revenue.
  • Operating leverage is strong. Non-GAAP operating margin reached 36.6%, up 180bp YoY, and incremental margin was ~41% (+$733M revenue, +~$304M operating income). In FY2026, revenue grew 42% while R&D grew 6.4%, so R&D fell from 33.9% to 25.3% of revenue.
  • Cash conversion is improving. FY2026 free cash flow was ~$1.40B. H1 FY2027 operating cash flow was already $1.24B, against $1.75B for all of FY2026. Morningstar models ROIC rising from 12% in FY2026 to 27.3% in FY2028E and 43% in FY2029E.
  • Customers are concentrated: the top 10 are 82% of revenue. The count of $100M+ customers is still widening, at 19 vs 13 in 2020. A new Tier 1 XPU customer ramps in FY2028, and Morningstar says design wins last 3-5 years, which makes revenue more contracted than a cyclical chip mix would suggest.

3. Signals from Q2 FY2027 and the investor day

  • Q2 revenue was $2.739B (+37% YoY). Data center was $2.172B (+46% YoY, +18% QoQ). The Q3 guide of $3.15B implies another ~15% sequential gain.
  • On Nov 2, the same day the deal was announced, management narrowed the Q3 range to ±2% and held the midpoint. The visibility it had going into the selloff has not changed.
  • The FY2028 revenue target rose twice in about two months, from $16.5B to $18B in August and then to $20B at the Oct 6 investor day. That is ~67% growth over the ~$12B FY2027 guide.
  • New product lines are adding up. Scale-out switching should be "well north of $1B" in FY2028. Marvell has a UALink switch win at a leading hyperscaler, and XConn adds ~$100M. Nvidia invested $2B in Marvell.

4. Why the feared scenarios already sit in the price

  • The price already reflects a bear case. At $171.41, the stock trades at Morgan Stanley's $172 bear value and 13.6x FY2029 consensus EPS of $12.62, against CFRA's 3-year average forward P/E of ~35x. At the memo's 18x bear multiple, the price implies FY2029 EPS of ~$9.52, about 25% below consensus.
  • The payoff is asymmetric. Using the memo's own scenarios, the bear case ($135) is 21% below the price, the base case ($196) is 14% above and the bull case ($330) is 93% above. The probability-weighted value of ~$205 is about 20% above the price.
  • Consensus already includes a capex slowdown. UBS models 2028 capex growth of +6% and Goldman +12%, after +76% in 2026. As of Nov 3, no hyperscaler has cut its 2027 capex. Marvell's FY2029 growth depends more on custom ramps already tied to design wins (>$12B) and on more optics per server than on total capex rising.
  • The fixed 4.5x exchange ratio means Lumentum holders take 33% of any post-close drop in laser margins. The deal also raises margins: Lumentum's 42.3% operating margin is above Marvell's 36.6%. MS's CY2029 pro forma EPS of $20.27 is ~3% accretive to consensus, which puts MRVL at ~8.5x that figure.
  • The selling looks mechanical. LITE trades about 11% below its implied deal value, and Needham says many LITE holders do not want to become MRVL holders. Even the downgrades set targets at or above the current price: TD Cowen $175, BofA $225, Cantor $225. Marvell's past deals (Cavium, Inphi, Avera) all beat their synergy targets, and per Morgan Stanley, Inphi's business grew more than 10x after a deal that looked expensive.

Summary. After a 32% three-day drop, MRVL trades at Morgan Stanley's bear value and 13.6x FY2029 consensus EPS. That is the valuation of a company in a downturn, yet guidance was just raised and Q3 visibility was reaffirmed. The capex-digestion fear is mostly already in consensus, which models 2028 capex growth of +6% to +12%. Marvell's growth from then on rests on custom ramps tied to signed design wins and on more optics per server. Using the memo's own scenarios, the downside to $135 is about 21% and the probability-weighted value of ~$205 is about 20% above the price. Those odds favor owning the stock at $171, with the Q3 report in early December as the next check.

Bear Case

1. AI capex digestion in 2027-28

  • Hyperscalers are spending more than they generate. UBP puts 2026 capex at ~$820B against ~$750B of operating cash flow, a $70B gap. For 2027 it is $1.0-1.3T against ~$900B, a gap of $100-400B that has to be borrowed. Meta posted its first negative FCF quarter (-$2.1B) and its spending commitments rose $125B in one quarter, from ~$700B to ~$825B.
  • Capex growth slows sharply just as Marvell's guide needs it most. UBS models capex +76% in 2026, +25% in 2027 and +6% in 2028. Goldman has +54% then +12%. Marvell's guide needs FY2028 revenue up 67% ($12B to $20B) and a 55-60% CAGR to FY2031. FY2029 is roughly CY2028, the year UBS has capex growing 6%.
  • Optical demand looks double-ordered. Lumentum is sold out through early CY2029 with 70% of next year's demand unmet. Consensus implies a ~$100B 2027 transceiver market against TD Cowen's $47B, a 2.1x gap. Inventory is already ~126 days, and MS models it rising from $1.4B to $5.2B by FY2029, a 3.7x build.
  • The 2022 precedent hurts more now. In the last digestion year FY2024 revenue fell 7% and the stock fell 57%. Data center was 40% of revenue in FY2024 and is 79% now. The memo's bear case keeps FY2029 EPS at $7.50, flat with FY2028's $7.35, and prices it at 18x for $135 (-21%). Ulf Huse estimates capex per GW could fall 50% on 10-20% lower demand.

2. Competition and pricing

  • Broadcom lands on the 1.6T cycle. Its Taurus 3nm 1.6T DSP (400G/lane) reaches mass production in late 2026, while Marvell's Ara is ramping. Electro-optics is $37.5B of management's $80B FY2031 target (47%), so that target depends on keeping DSP share against Broadcom.
  • The Lumentum deal creates a neutrality problem. China-based makers supply 60-65% of transceivers, over 40% of Marvell revenue ships to China (CFRA), and one distributor took 37% of FY2026 revenue. Marvell now owns a competitor to those customers. MS calls supplier neutrality "the main scale-out risk," and I found no direct statement of support from any module maker.
  • Custom silicon dilutes margins and gets re-bid each generation. Custom runs ~50% GM (Morningstar) against 58.9% corporate, and Q3 GM guidance of 57.5-58.5% is already 40-140bp lower. The top 10 customers are 82% of revenue, so losing one AWS or Microsoft generation would knock a large hole in the >$12B FY2029 custom target.
  • Astera is the favorite on UALink/PCIe. MS says Trainium 4 is Astera's "to lose." Marvell's own entry there, XConn, is guided to ~$100M in FY2028, 0.5% of the $20B guide.

3. Inorganic revenue and per-share math

  • The organic revenue story mostly holds up. The auto Ethernet business sold to Infineon was expected to bring in $225-250M in FY2026 (Infineon release). Removing ~$0.12B from the FY2026 base moves FY2027 growth only from +46.5% to ~+48.7%. The bear point is earnings quality: the $1.8B GAAP gain equals 73% of FY2026 non-GAAP net income ($2.47B), and it does not recur.
  • The acquisitions add little revenue in FY2028 but real dilution. If Celestial ramps in a straight line to its $500M exit run rate, FY2028 revenue is ~$0.25B (my estimate). With XConn's $0.1B that is ~$0.35B, or 1.75% of $20B. For it, Marvell paid $3.79B ($3.25B plus $540M). Celestial's terms were $1B cash plus ~27.2M shares, and an earnout pays up to ~27.2M more shares if cumulative revenue reaches $2B by FY2029 (MarketChameleon). That is up to ~54M shares, ~6% of 907M.
  • Lumentum lowers revenue per share. On MS numbers, standalone CY2029 revenue is ~$54B ($71.0B less ~$17B) on 925M shares, or $58.4 per share. Pro forma is $71.0B on 1,390M shares, or $51.1, which is 12.5% lower. Revenue rises 31% while shares rise 50%.
  • Without synergies the deal is about 8% dilutive. EPS goes from $21.55 standalone to $20.27 pro forma (-5.9%). Take out MS's $700M of synergies (pre-tax, and I assume a 15% tax rate) and EPS drops another $0.43 to ~$19.84, about 7.9% dilutive. Those synergies are unspecified and partly come from eliminating sales Marvell already makes to Lumentum.

4. Multiple compression

  • Flat FY2029 EPS. At $7.35, 18x gives $132 (-23%) and 15x gives $110 (-36%).
  • Consensus cut 30-40%. That takes FY2029 EPS from $12.62 to $8.83-7.57. At 15x the stock is worth $132-114 (-23% to -33%). At 18x it is worth $159-136.
  • The memo's $205 depends on its bull case. The bull case puts 20% weight on $330, which needs FY2029 EPS 19% above consensus. Drop it and reweight the bear and base cases 30/70: 0.3×$135 + 0.7×$196 = $178, 4% above $171.41.
  • Lumentum margins are above its own target model. Today it runs 53.2% GM and 42.3% OM against a 49-52% / 38-42% target, after gaining 2,000bp of gross margin in 8 quarters, mostly from pricing. If its ~$17B of CY2029 revenue earns 4 points less operating margin, EPS falls $0.42 to $19.85. If half the pricing gain reverses (-1,000bp), EPS falls $1.04 to $19.23, 10.8% below standalone. MS calls laser margins "potentially more cyclical than base MRVL."
  • There are also deal-break and full-bust costs. Fees owed by Marvell total $5.8B plus $3.2B regulatory, or $9.9 per share (~6% of market cap). LITE trades ~11% below the deal-implied ~$790. The tail case is a repeat of 2022: -57% from $171 is $74, close to the $70.69 52-week low.

Summary

The main risk is the calendar. Marvell's guide needs revenue up 67% in FY2028, while Street models have hyperscaler capex growth falling to 6-12% in CY2028 and that capex already exceeds operating cash flow by $100-400B. A flat FY2029 at 15-18x gives $110-132, and the memo's own value without its bull case is ~$178, so $171 offers little margin of safety. The Lumentum deal cuts per-share revenue by 12.5%, adds a business earning above its own target margins, and strains relationships with China module makers that supply 60-65% of the transceiver market. My bear target is $110-135, with $74 as the tail if 2022 repeats.

Bear's notes: the inorganic adjustment does not hurt organic growth (the auto Ethernet sale lowered reported growth slightly); MS does not disclose Lumentum margins in its pro forma, so the margin-normalization numbers are a sensitivity; the 15% tax rate and straight-line Celestial ramp are assumptions; MarketScreener gives 24.6M Celestial shares issued at close vs 27.2M at signing. Sources: MarketChameleon, MarketScreener, BusinessWire.

Orchestrator corrections (both analysts' text above is left as written)

  1. Break fees are alternatives. Under the November 2 merger 8-K, Marvell owes $5.8B if its board changes its recommendation or it takes a superior proposal, and $3.2B if the deal fails on regulatory grounds. It would not pay both, so the regulatory-break exposure is $3.2B (~$3.5 per share, ~2% of market cap), not $9.9 per share.
  2. GAAP gain vs non-GAAP base. The $1.8B gain on the auto Ethernet sale inflated FY2026 GAAP EPS ($3.07). The non-GAAP net income it is compared with ($2.47B) already excludes it, so the gain does not flatter the earnings base the valuations use.

Arbitrator Assessment

The arbitrator checked both reports against the equity report and memo before ruling. One edit by the orchestrator: the arbitrator wrote FY2028 EPS as "~$7.45"; consensus is $7.35 (CFRA, Oct 29), so the FY2029 consensus of $12.62 implies ~72% growth. The figures below use $7.35.

Corrections and checks

  1. Break fees (bear error). The $5.8B and $3.2B fees cover different termination triggers and would never both be paid. A regulatory failure is the realistic break, so the exposure is $3.2B (~$3.5 per share, ~2% of market cap), not $9.9 per share. The memo also expects a break to lift MRVL, so it may not be a net cost to holders at all.
  2. Auto Ethernet gain (bear error). The $1.8B gain sits in GAAP income. The $2.47B non-GAAP base already excludes it, so the earnings-quality point does not reach the valuation.
  3. The $178 "no bull case" value (bear sensitivity). The arithmetic is right, but zeroing the bull case goes too far: Morgan Stanley ($14.32) and Morningstar ($15.78) already sit near its ~$15 EPS. Its weak point is the 22x multiple during an 18-month overhang. Reweighting 35/55/10 gives ~$188 and 40/45/15 gives ~$192. Fair weighted value: ~$185-205, 8-20% upside.
  4. "Price implies FY2029 EPS of ~$9.52" (bull). The arithmetic is right, but the reasoning is circular: at 13.6x the same price implies the full $12.62. The price is not evidence that a bear case is priced in.
  5. "~8.5x pro forma" (bull). $20.27 is a CY2029 figure about three years out. It assumes a close the memo puts at ~60% and includes $700M of synergies nobody has specified. The comparable multiple is 13.6x standalone FY2029. Against MS's own standalone ($21.55), the deal is ~6% dilutive, not 3% accretive.

Other problems the arbitrator found:

  • Bear, inventory. The 3.7x inventory build is not evidence of double ordering: MS models inventory days flat at 115-119, so inventory just tracks revenue.
  • Bear, revenue per share. The -12.5% revenue-per-share figure is the wrong metric for a higher-margin acquisition; EPS dilution is the right one.
  • Bear, Celestial earnout. The extra ~27M earnout shares are paid only in the success case.
  • Bull, gross margin. The bull says gross margin "held," but the Q3 guide is 40-140bp below Q2's 58.9%.
  • Bull, fixed exchange ratio. MRVL holders take 67% of any post-close drop in laser margins, not just the 33% that falls on Lumentum holders.
  • Bull, deal and margins. The "deal raises margins" claim rests on Lumentum margins that sit above its own target model.

The 3 Critical Divergence Assumptions

1. FY2029 (CY2028) earnings keep growing while hyperscaler capex growth slows to single digits

  • Bull version: FY2029 growth comes from signed custom ramps (>$12B custom), a new Tier 1 XPU customer, and more optics per server, which decouple Marvell from the capex growth rate. No hyperscaler has cut 2027 capex, and the Q3 midpoint was held on deal day.
  • Bear version: 2027 capex already exceeds hyperscaler operating cash flow by $100-400B. Growth falls to 6-12% in CY2028, and data center is now 79% of revenue against 40% in the FY2024 digestion, when revenue fell 7% and the stock 57%. FY2029 EPS stalls near FY2028.
  • Why it's the crux: Every valuation in both reports is a multiple of FY2029 EPS. The gap between $7.50 and $12.62 is worth more than everything else in dispute combined.
  • Verdict weight: Lean bear, moderate. Consensus does not "already include" the slowdown. FY2029 consensus of $12.62 implies ~72% EPS growth over FY2028's $7.35 in a year UBS has capex growing 6%. Marvell gains share, so this is possible, but consensus is not conservative. Custom ramps come out of the same customers' capex budgets. The bull does hold the near-term facts: Q3 visibility, no 2027 cuts, and two guidance raises in two months. That keeps the lean moderate rather than strong. The memo's 30% bear weight looks slightly low and its 20% bull weight slightly high.

2. The Lumentum deal creates value for MRVL holders at today's terms

  • Bull version: The fixed 4.5x ratio shares the downside with Lumentum holders. Lumentum's margins are above Marvell's. Marvell beat synergy targets on Cavium, Inphi and Avera, and Inphi grew more than 10x after a deal that looked expensive. The selloff is arbitrage selling.
  • Bear version: Marvell pays for peak laser margins with ~50% more shares. EPS is 6% below standalone with synergies and ~8% below without; if half of Lumentum's pricing gain reverses, ~11% below.
  • Why it's the crux: If the deal closes, it sets per-share earnings from CY2028 onward. Until then, the LITE spread and the overhang cap the multiple the bull case needs.
  • Verdict weight: Lean bear, moderate. Morgan Stanley, the bull's main source, has the deal ~6% dilutive against its own standalone. Lumentum's margins sit above the top of its target band, and the bull's margin-accretion argument assumes they last. The bear's sensitivities check out arithmetically (-$0.42 for 4pt of OM, -$1.04 for 10pt of GM). The break-fee and revenue-per-share errors do not change the direction, because EPS dilution decides it.

3. Marvell keeps merchant DSP share through 1.6T and 3.2T despite Broadcom and the neutrality conflict

  • Bull version: The 3nm Ara ships in volume while Broadcom's Taurus reaches mass production only in late 2026. Microsoft sole-sources Marvell optical chips for its North American regional data centers, and design wins last 3-5 years.
  • Bear version: Electro-optics is 47% of the $80B FY2031 target. China-based module makers that compete with Lumentum supply 60-65% of transceivers, and none has stated support. MS calls neutrality "the main scale-out risk."
  • Why it's the crux: DSPs are Marvell's highest-margin franchise and the part of the moat the deal puts at risk. Losing share would hit gross margin and the FY2031 plan together.
  • Verdict weight: Lean bull, narrow. For the FY2029 valuation year, the bull's evidence is stronger: Marvell is first to volume at 1.6T, and those awards are largely made. No share loss has been observed, and the bear's case rests on a missing statement and a long-dated target. The risk falls on 3.2T awards decided while the deal is pending, which mostly affect FY2030 and later. The verdict flips if InnoLight or Eoptolink names Broadcom as primary at 3.2T.

To own MRVL, an investor has to be right that FY2029 (CY2028) EPS grows well above FY2028's ~$7.35 in a year Street models have hyperscaler capex growing only 6-12%.
