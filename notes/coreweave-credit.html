Why CoreWeave pays 15% to borrow

CoreWeave's 9.75% notes due 2031 were issued at 102 cents in April. By late October they traded near 81 cents, yielding over 15%. Its five-year CDS hit a record 1,194.5bp on 15 October. This page sets out what the bond market is pricing, what would have to line up for a default, and which events could set one off.

Short answer: mostly timing and funding, with a margin question underneath.

CoreWeave spends cash on GPUs and data centers 12 to 36 months before those assets earn revenue. In the meantime it pays interest every quarter, so it has to raise new money continuously. Bond buyers are not worried about the backlog. They are worried about whether the capital markets stay open long enough for that backlog to turn into cash. Underneath that sits a slower question: whether GPU rental rates stay above the cost of the debt that bought the GPUs.

Where the market puts it

CoreWeave prices closer to CCC than to its own rating

What 1,100bp implies

A rough rule converts a CDS spread into an annual default probability: spread ÷ (1 − recovery). Assuming 40% recovery, 1,194.5bp ÷ 0.6 ≈ 20% a year. Compounded over five years, 1 − e−1.0 ≈ 63%. That matches Simon Ree's reading of "about 60% within five years". The market is not calling default certain. It is pricing default as more likely than not.

Why secured debt is so much cheaper

In March, CoreWeave borrowed $8.5B at SOFR + 225bp with an A3 rating because the loan sat directly behind one customer's contract. Its unsecured notes have no such claim. They get paid only after the asset-level lenders, out of whatever cash is left at the parent. The 15% yield prices that residual position. It does not price the company's best customers.

How the other neoclouds compare

The peers took the same equity hit in October. CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN each fell about 20% in the week of 13 Oct, and on 22 Oct TeraWulf fell 11.3%, IREN 8.4% and Nebius 7.2%. Their debt has held up much better, because they fund the build differently. CoreWeave leans on unsecured high-yield bonds. Nebius and IREN use customer prepayments, convertibles and contract-secured loans. Applied Digital and Cipher issue secured bonds backed by long hyperscaler leases.

New issue math

Why a new unsecured bond has to pay 10% or more

A bond's yield is roughly the Treasury yield plus the credit spread. The Treasury part is about 5% today; the 10-year traded between 4.96% and 5.19% in mid-October. That leaves room for less than 500bp of spread below 10%. CoreWeave's unsecured spread is twice that. Before the selloff, in April, its 9.75% coupon notes still priced near a 9.4% yield. Secured debt that sits behind a single customer contract costs much less. The peer rows at the top of the chart show it trading at 6–8.5%.

Timing

Cash goes out years before it comes back

The timeline below follows one illustrative new cluster. The pressure comes from where the bars sit. Capex and interest start on day one. Revenue starts only when the site is energized. The cheapest debt is secured on long contracts, while new contracts run 3 to 6 months. And the GPUs may lose their earning power before they are written off.

Funding

The loop that worries bondholders

A company that pays interest from new financing depends on the cost of that financing. CoreWeave pays interest and funds capex with fresh capital, so its share price and its bond yields feed each other. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid called this "a self-fulfilling prophecy". Each step below has already happened at least once in October.

Default mechanics

What has to line up for a default

Wide spreads alone don't cause a default. A company defaults when it has an obligation due and cannot find the cash. For CoreWeave, that means all four conditions below have to hold at the same time. If any one fails, the company can most likely refinance, restructure quietly, or sell assets.

Most likely form

Distressed exchange

Bondholders swap into new paper with a later maturity, a lower coupon, or equity. Rating agencies count this as a default even though no payment is missed. When companies with this mix of secured and unsecured debt default, this is usually how.

Fastest path

Asset-level acceleration

A customer cancels or renegotiates the contract a GPU loan is secured on. That breaches the loan's terms, the lender accelerates, and cross-default terms in the indentures may pull the notes in. Each of those depends on terms we have not read.

Least likely

Plain missed coupon

Simply running out of cash on a payment date would require every channel to be closed for several quarters, with no backstop. Management has many options to use before that happens.

The other side

Why the bulls hold the bonds

  • The backlog is real and mostly investment grade. $104B, about two-thirds owed by Microsoft, Meta, IBM and similar counterparties. The CEO says it "more than supports all of our debt obligations."
  • Compute is scarce right now. New contracts sign at about $40M per MW, 4 to 6 times last year. CoreWeave says it has sold nearly all its 2027 capacity.
  • The debt is mostly secured on contracts. Asset-level loans are matched to customer payments. Default risk is concentrated in the unsecured notes, which are a minority of the stack.
  • Strategic value. 1.5 GW running today and 4.2 GW planned. A hyperscaler, Nvidia or a private-equity consortium would likely buy the platform before letting it liquidate.
