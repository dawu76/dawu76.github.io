1. Executive Summary

Thesis

Marvell owns the leading merchant optical DSP franchise and a custom-silicon business that now serves AWS, Microsoft, Google and a new Tier 1 customer. Together they put it on the fastest-growing part of AI hardware: moving data between accelerators. Revenue is guided from $8.2B (FY2026) to ~$20B (FY2028), with a $70-90B FY2031 target. The stock fell from $251.65 to $171.41 in three sessions after Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum for $115B in stock. At $171 the market pays ~23x FY2028 and ~13.6x FY2029 consensus EPS, below CFRA's 3- and 5-year average forward P/E of ~35x and ~32x and equal to Morgan Stanley's bear-case price ($172). The selloff prices in a lot of deal damage. It does not price in a 2027-28 AI capex digestion, which is the larger risk. A position makes sense, sized for a further 30% fall.

Bull case ($330, 15%): Scale-up optics and custom XPU attach ramp on plan, FY2029 EPS reaches ~$15 (Morgan Stanley $14.32, Morningstar $15.78), and the market looks through the deal and pays 22x once the arbitrage overhang fades. The EPS is plausible; the 22x is the weak part while the deal is pending.

Bear case ($135, 35%): Hyperscalers digest capacity in 2027-28, optical prices normalize as shortages end, FY2029 EPS stalls near $7.50 (roughly the FY2028 level), and Marvell is locked into buying Lumentum at peak laser margins with ~50% more shares.

Base case ($196, 50%): Growth continues but below the investor-day path; FY2029 EPS ~$11.50 (~9% under consensus) at 17x.

Target investor: Growth investor with a 2-3 year horizon who can hold through a 30-40% drawdown. Not for income (0.1% yield) or value investors needing a margin of safety on normalized earnings.

Why this memo is ~35-45% below the $300-350 cluster:

  1. Multiple. The bulls apply 28-46x to CY2027-28 EPS, anchored to Marvell's 32-35x historical average. Those averages come from years when earnings were depressed and growth lay ahead. CY2028 earnings, if they arrive, will be close to peak-cycle earnings for optics, where Lumentum says components are sold out through early CY2029. Peak earnings deserve a mid-teens multiple, and the market is already setting ~13-14x.
  2. Overhang. With a fixed exchange ratio, merger arbitrage funds buy LITE and short 4.5 MRVL shares each, for 18 months. Deals that take this long (outside date can run to August 1, 2028) tend to cap the acquirer's multiple.
  3. Estimates. The base case runs ~9% below consensus FY2029 EPS to allow for optical price normalization and some DSP share loss at module makers that compete with Lumentum. Consensus is not conservative: $12.62 is ~72% above FY2028's $7.35, in a year UBS models hyperscaler capex growth at 6% (Goldman 12%).

The firms closer to this memo (TD Cowen $175, BofA and Cantor $225) cite the same three reasons.

Deal-inclusive check. Morgan Stanley's pro forma CY2029 EPS is $20.27 on 1,390M shares (including $700M of synergies). At 12x that is ~$243, ~$194 discounted two years at 12%; at 15x, ~$242 discounted. So if the combined estimates hold, the deal does not by itself make $171 expensive. This is weak support. The $20.27 is about three years out, includes $700M of synergies nobody has itemized, and needs a close this memo puts at ~60%. Against Morgan Stanley's own standalone CY2029 EPS of $21.55 the deal is ~6% dilutive (~8% without synergies). Use the standalone 13.6x FY2029 multiple, not a pro forma multiple, when judging cheapness.

Geography: over 40% ships to China, mostly to non-Chinese customers' factories (CFRA). End demand is 50-60% North America (Morgan Stanley). Customers: one distributor 37% and one direct customer 14% of FY2026 revenue; top ten 82% (10-K).

Unit economics

Marvell sells chips, so unit economics are content per system and margin per program:

  • Optical content per XPU: management expects "thousands of dollars per XPU" across both ends of each scale-up link once NPO/CPO ramps, from almost nothing today (Morgan Stanley, Oct 7). Each 1.6T transceiver carries one DSP plus TIAs and drivers.
  • Gross margin by line: custom ~50% (Morningstar estimate); optical DSPs and switching above the ~58-59% corporate average. Long-term gross margin guide is 56-59% as custom grows.
  • Custom economics: lower gross margin is offset by NRE and customer co-investment booked against opex, so custom is operating-margin neutral to accretive (Morningstar).
  • R&D per design win: leading-edge custom programs cost hundreds of millions to develop and run 3-5 years. The 20+ active custom wins and 19 customers above $100M (up from 13 in 2020) are what keep R&D at ~25% of revenue workable.

Operating leverage

Q2 FY2027 vs Q2 FY2026: revenue rose $733M ($2,006M to $2,739M), and non-GAAP operating income rose ~$304M (from ~$698M at 34.8% to ~$1,002M at 36.6%), an incremental margin of ~41%. Management's FY2031 target model (44-46% operating margin, opex growing at half the revenue rate) implies incremental margins near 47%. Morgan Stanley models 41.4% in CY2027; Morningstar 48% by FY2031.

After the deal, leverage cuts both ways: Lumentum's fab-heavy cost base (capex ~15% of revenue in FY2026) raises incremental margins while fabs fill and hurts them when utilization falls.

Working capital and cash conversion

  • Inventory: ~126 days in FY2026; Morgan Stanley models 115-119 days through FY2029, with inventory growing from $1.4B to $5.2B.
  • Cash conversion: FY2026 FCF ~$1.40B vs ~$2.47B non-GAAP net income (~57%). Stock comp ($591M) and inventory build explain most of the gap. Morningstar forecasts negative FCF to the firm in FY2027 (-$742M, including the Celestial AI payment), then $3.9B in FY2028.
  • Cash cycle: receivable days are not disclosed in the sources used; with ~120 inventory days, the cycle is long for a fabless company and will absorb cash as revenue grows ~60% a year.

Moat classification

Primary: IP and design scale. Leading-edge SerDes, DSP, silicon photonics and packaging under one roof, built over a decade with acquisitions (Cavium, Avera, Inphi, Innovium, Celestial AI). Secondary: switching costs. Customers design their networks around Marvell's feature sets, and custom chips are co-designed for years. Morningstar rates the moat narrow.

Durability

  • For: non-GAAP operating margin rising to 36.6% while R&D falls as a share of revenue; ROIC rising from 12% (FY2026) toward 27% (FY2028E, Morningstar); repeat wins across four hyperscalers; Google chose Marvell as its second custom partner despite Broadcom's incumbency.
  • Against: every speed generation is a new contest; Broadcom is larger and earns higher margins; custom sockets get re-bid each generation; linear-drive and CPO designs could reduce the number of discrete DSPs per link over time.
  • New risk from the deal: Marvell's DSP lead depends on selling to all module makers, and most transceivers come from China-based makers that compete with Lumentum. Management says module makers have voiced support. Morgan Stanley finds the precedents (Marvell selling DCI modules while supplying coherent DSPs to rivals) "encouraging but not conclusive," since DCI is a much smaller market.

Assessment: narrow and dependent on execution. It holds as long as Marvell is first or second to each new speed node. The next DSP award cycle (3.2T, awarded during the deal's pendency) is the test.

4. Top 3 Growth Drivers & Sensitivities

Driver 1: Optical interconnect (scale-out speed upgrades plus scale-up optics)

  • Description: 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T DSP cycles in scale-out; NPO and CPO replacing copper inside racks (scale-up), where Marvell sells DSPs, TIAs, drivers, NPO engines and Celestial AI's photonic fabric.
  • TAM: interconnect TAM ~$65B in CY2030 (investor day). Company target: $37.5B of FY2031 revenue.
  • Milestones: scale-up optics "multiple hundreds of millions" in FY2028; Celestial AI CPO at a $500M run rate by Q4 FY2028 and $1B by Q4 FY2029; Nvidia Rubin Ultra (2027) copper vs CPO choice; Feynman (2028) fully CPO per CFRA.
  • FY2029 revenue: bear $9B / base $14B / bull $18B.

Driver 2: Custom XPU and XPU attach

  • Description: full XPUs (Trainium, Maia) plus attach chips (NICs, CXL memory controllers, storage controllers, inference accelerators) for AWS, Microsoft, Google and a new Tier 1 customer.
  • TAM: custom TAM ~$235B in CY2030 (investor day). Google framework up to $120B cumulative through FY2033.
  • Milestones: custom more than doubles in FY2028; >$12B in FY2029; Google warrant vesting (one tranche per $500M of purchases); Microsoft Maia volume from FY2028 (Morningstar).
  • FY2029 revenue: bear $6B / base $11B / bull $14B.

Driver 3: Lumentum combination and co-designed optics (from 1H CY2028)

  • Description: lasers (200G EMLs, UHP CW lasers for CPO, 70-80% share in pump lasers), optical circuit switches and InP fabs added to Marvell's electronics. Combined TAM $550B (company).
  • Economics: $1B+ run-rate COGS synergies (Morgan Stanley assumes $700M in CY2029); Lumentum's second NPO/CPO win (>$5B, shipping 2H CY2027); priority access to scarce InP capacity.
  • Milestones: S-4 filing; shareholder votes; HSR and China (SAMR) approvals; outside date November 1, 2027, extendable to August 1, 2028; next DSP design awards at non-Lumentum module makers.
  • Probability of closing: ~60% (estimate). Morningstar moved from "below 50%" (Oct 29, talks only) to 75% (Nov 2, signed terms). The $3.2B regulatory break fee shows Marvell's commitment; China review is the main obstacle and the reason this memo stays below Morningstar.
  • Impact: none on FY2029 standalone numbers. Morgan Stanley's CY2029 pro forma EPS is $20.27 vs its $21.55 standalone (-6%) and $19.76 consensus (+3%).

1. AI capex digestion

  • Description: hyperscaler capex now exceeds operating cash flow (~$820B capex vs ~$750B OCF in 2026, per UBP). Huse argues 2027's ~$1.75T of AI capex needs ~$700B of new revenue that does not exist, forcing cuts.
  • Trigger: a major hyperscaler cuts its 2027 capex guide; neocloud financing fails (see crwv-credit-squeeze-20261028); optical double-ordering surfaces.
  • Impact: FY2029 EPS toward $7.50, stock $110-135. In 2022 the stock fell 57%. Data center is now 79% of revenue against ~40% before the FY2024 digestion, when revenue fell 7%. The stress test ranks this the crux (lean bear, moderate): it moves value between ~$135 and ~$227, more than the deal and DSP questions combined.
  • Mitigation: custom programs carry multi-year volume commitments; inference demand is broader than training. Investor mitigation: small position, staged buys.
  • Midterms (added 2026-11-06): the November 3 results put candidates who promised data-center pauses into the Texas and Georgia governor's offices. Texas holds ~27% of planned US data-center GW. The wiki's sizing (midterms-data-center-capex-sizing-2026-11) puts ~$50B (~6%) of 2027 US AI capex at risk of slipping, with ~$35B lost for good, most of it in site work and power. For Marvell the demand is more likely moved than lost: while powered capacity is short, chips and optics go wherever a shell is energized, including abroad. The near-term hit is to the multiple (SOX fell 12% on November 5). It becomes an EPS risk if the states that would take relocated projects (Ohio, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Louisiana) add their own limits, or Georgia's pause runs past mid-2027. None of the six MRVL analyst notes in the library addresses the election; all predate it. Watch for it in Q3 FY2027 previews.

2. Lumentum overpay and overhang

  • Description: Marvell pays ~22x Lumentum's annualized revenue and ~38x FY2026 revenue for margins that sit above Lumentum's own target model on tight InP supply.
  • Trigger: Lumentum gross margin falls back below 50%; Chinese EML capacity arrives; VCSEL-based scale-up takes share.
  • Impact: Morgan Stanley already has the deal ~6% dilutive against its standalone CY2029 EPS; if half of Lumentum's pricing gain reverses, ~11%. Goodwill impairment later; arbitrage shorting weighs on the stock to close. With 67% of the combined company, MRVL holders take most of any post-close fall in laser margins. Stress test verdict: lean bear, moderate.
  • Mitigation: fixed ratio means Marvell pays less in dollars if both stocks fall; Marvell's record with Cavium, Inphi and Avera, which all beat synergy targets (Morgan Stanley).

3. DSP neutrality

  • Description: module makers that compete with Lumentum shift 1.6T/3.2T DSP volume to Broadcom or others.
  • Trigger: next-generation DSP awards at InnoLight, Eoptolink and others go to rivals.
  • Impact: even modest share loss in the largest interconnect line; Morgan Stanley calls it "the main scale-out risk." Timing matters: Marvell's 3nm 1.6T DSP ships in volume while Broadcom's reaches mass production in late 2026, so FY2029 is mostly protected. The exposure is 3.2T awards decided while the deal is pending, which hit FY2030 and later. Stress test verdict: lean bull, narrow.
  • Mitigation: management commits to selling across the ecosystem; Lumentum already sells lasers to most rival module makers.

4. China

  • Description: the deal needs Chinese approval while over 40% of Marvell's revenue ships to China and 60-65% of transceivers come from China-based vendors.
  • Trigger: SAMR delays or conditions approval; export controls escalate.
  • Impact: $3.2B regulatory break fee (~$3.5 a share, ~2% of market cap); the $5.8B fee covers other triggers and would not be paid on top of it. Remedies limiting DSP sales; tariffs under Section 232.
  • Mitigation: little product overlap between the two companies; long outside date. A break would probably lift MRVL shares.

5. Custom program loss

  • Description: a hyperscaler moves the next XPU generation to Broadcom, MediaTek or in-house.
  • Trigger: reports of a Trainium or Maia generation loss; Google warrant vesting slower than expected.
  • Impact: custom is the biggest swing factor in FY2029 revenue ($6-14B range).
  • Mitigation: 20+ design wins and four hyperscalers reduce single-program exposure.

Pre-mortem

If this investment fails by late 2029, the most likely reason is that AI data center spending peaked in 2027, optical prices and volumes fell together, and Marvell closed the Lumentum deal in 2028 just as laser margins normalized. It would then own a fab business bought at the top, with ~50% more shares and earnings below FY2028 levels. The stock would sit at a mid-teens multiple of shrinking EPS.

Assumptions that must hold

  1. Hyperscaler capex grows through CY2028 (management assumes ~$3T of data center capex by CY2030), and Marvell's FY2029 EPS grows well above FY2028's ~$7.35 even if capex growth slows to 6-12%. This is the assumption that decides the investment.
  2. Marvell keeps first-or-second position in 1.6T and 3.2T DSPs, including at module makers that compete with Lumentum.
  3. Custom revenue more than doubles in FY2028 and passes $12B in FY2029.
  4. Scale-up optics ramps from 2H CY2027 on Nvidia's and others' roadmaps.
  5. Lumentum's margins do not fall sharply before closing.
  6. Non-GAAP operating margin reaches ~40% in FY2028.

Action plan

Size the whole position so that a fall to $110-135 is survivable. Price levels set the most you pay; the evidence gates decide whether you add.

  • Now ($171-185): starter position (~1/4), matching the first tranche in mrvl-lumentum-deal-20261102.
  • Second tranche (~1/3), early December: add if the Q3 results pass KPI 1 (Q4 guide ≥ 12% QoQ) and KPI 3 (FY2028 ≥ $20B), at any price up to ~$185. Below $150-160 without those, hold.
  • Third tranche, after late January-early February: add if KPI 9 is green (Big-5 2027 capex ≥ $1.0T, no one guiding down). If KPIs 3 and 9 are red, do not add even at $120-130; that is the bear case playing out, worth ~$135 or less.
  • Sooner: signs the deal will break, or a clean Q3 with a raised FY2028 outlook.
  • Exit or cut: FY2028 guide below $18B together with a hyperscaler capex cut.
