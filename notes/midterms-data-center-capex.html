Summary

The election defers roughly $15-125B of 2027 US AI capex, base case ~$50B (~6%). Most of it is a 6-12 month delay or a move to another state. About $35B is lost for good.

What changed on November 3

The two states that matter most for US AI capacity elected governors who promised pauses, and Democrats won both chambers of Congress.

Texas governorGina Hinojosa beat Greg Abbott; takes office January 19. Pledged a moratorium on all data-center approvals until the legislature acts, plus ending tax breaks.Texas is ~27% of planned US data-center GW (Wells Fargo).

Four ways to size the capex hit

The methods land on ~5-6% of 2027 US AI capex deferred and ~1-2% lost for good, mostly in buildings and power gear.

Assumptions: US AI capex 2027 ~$800B (all buyers); ~28 GW of US capacity added in 2028; ~$12B/GW for shell, power and cooling (spent 12-24 months before power-on) and ~$35B/GW for IT (spent 0-6 months before). SOX's -12% matches a one-year push-out at 12-13%, not cancellation. Moves of -20% or worse (STRL, MOD, HUT, CIFR) imply a two-year delay or a permanent cut. Hyperscalers' +3-7% on day one priced the FCF benefit of lower capex.

GLJ's 13 GW Texas figure × $47B/GW ≈ $610B is the size of the Texas pipeline, not a one-year cut; it overstates near-term capex by ~5-10x.

Sensitivity: a Tier A not-started cut of ×0.4 loses 18.5 GW in Tier A alone; ×0.8 loses 7.1 GW. National range ~10-22 GW. 16 GW × ~$47B/GW ≈ $750B of project value over several years; if half relocates, ~$380B net.

The swing factor is the 20.7 GW already under construction in Texas and Georgia. If Hinojosa's order covers started projects, 2027-28 deliveries take a much larger hit. Hut 8, Cipher, Galaxy (Helios, leased to CoreWeave) and IREN (2 GW Sweetwater) hold those sites.

The eight levers

  1. Delay or cancellation. A one-year delay costs ~10% at an 11% discount rate; cancellation hits terminal value.
  2. Position in the build sequence. Site work and power gear lose orders 12-24 months before GPUs would be installed.
  3. Fungibility. Chips can ship to another site; a Texas substation contract cannot.
  4. Geographic concentration in Texas or Georgia.
  5. Leverage and financing. A small rent delay becomes a large equity loss.
  6. Orders versus backlog. Backlog protects revenue, but the multiple follows orders.
  7. Scarcity value of energized MW and grid-connected generation, the main source of winners.
  8. Who pays for grid upgrades. Developers absorbing costs is a margin story; regulators blocking projects is a volume story.

What to watch

  • Whether Hinojosa's order exempts projects already under construction (largest single swing factor)
  • Whether the ERCOT audit ends in December with the freeze lifted before January 19
  • Bottoms' Georgia order: scope, duration, treatment of approved projects; Project Camellia (3.2 GW) vote November 17
  • Texas legislative session in January: tax-break repeal, large-load tariffs
  • Hyperscaler Q4 calls (late January): same capex total with a new state mix supports "relocate, not cancel"
  • New local bans in relocation states: Clarksville, AR bond votes December 1; Mandan, ND December 15; Ohio statewide amendment aimed at November 2027
  • VST Q3 (November 6) and CEG Q3 (November 9) commentary on ERCOT and PJM load
  • Vertiv's February report and first 2027 guide; GE Vernova orders
  • CoreWeave and Hut 8 disclosures on Texas site timing; CRWV CDS

Caveats

  • No source gives a formal national capex cut. UBS's 30% is capacity "at risk," not a cancellation forecast.
  • Bernstein's 244 GW was published before the results and includes many speculative projects.
  • Trackers disagree on the total (346 vs ~387 vs 433 GW). The $/GW split, completion rates and post-election cuts are my assumptions. Moving $/GW from $47B to $40B or $60B changes Method 2 by -15% / +28%.
  • Sources disagree on control of the Texas legislature.
  • Part of the Nov 5 selloff predates the election.
