Bottom line

Takeaway

Decide based on 2027 income. If it lands under the 400% FPL cliff (about $132,000 for a family of 4), stay on Kaiser. If it lands clearly over, switch to the AmeriBenefit $10K CAT plan, or to Copay $2,500 if someone needs regular specialist care.

Inputs and assumptions

Takeaway

Worst-case figures come straight from the plan documents. Healthy- and moderate-year figures are rough estimates of typical negotiated prices.

  • AmeriBenefit pricing: Family tier, 45–54 band (the broker outline uses it for ages 50–54), Tier 2. The tier appears to be health-rated. At Tier 1, CAT is $11,004/yr; at Tier 3, it's $14,752/yr. Copay is $13,020 at Tier 1 and $19,350 at Tier 3.
  • Kaiser 2027: $2,020.94/mo before help, then −$1,348.46 APTC and −$4 CA credit, for $668.48/mo. Cost sharing is unchanged: $5,800 / $11,600 deductible and $11,650 / $23,300 out-of-pocket max.
  • Plan version: Kaiser here means the Bronze 60 HMO, not the Bronze 60 HDHP. The HMO isn't HSA-qualified.
  • Healthy year: checkups, 6 sick or urgent-care visits, 2 lab draws, 1 X-ray, monthly generics.
  • Moderate year: the healthy year plus a child's fracture (ER, X-ray, 2 orthopedist visits, 6 PT visits) and one adult MRI.
  • Worst case: premium plus the in-network family out-of-pocket max.

Why CAT beats unsubsidized Kaiser even in light years

Takeaway

CAT does cost more for care in light years, but only $260–$1,100 more. Its premium is $11,831 lower, and in a bad year its family out-of-pocket max is $3,300 lower.

Premium$24,251$12,420CAT −$11,831
Out-of-pocket, healthy year~$940~$1,200CAT +$260
Out-of-pocket, moderate year~$5,600~$6,700CAT +$1,100
Out-of-pocket, worst case$23,300$20,000CAT −$3,300
  • Similar deductibles in practice. Kaiser Bronze 60 HMO also has a large deductible: $5,800 per person, then 40%. In light years most claims fall under the deductible on both plans.
  • The widest gap is about $2,500 per person. It happens around $10,000 of claims in one person's year: Kaiser comes to $7,480, CAT to $10,000. Above about $16,300 of claims per person, Kaiser costs more.
  • Brand-name drugs are the exception. CAT excludes them, and those costs have no ceiling.

$10K CAT default pick

Lowest cost in all three modeled years. Once one person reaches $10,000, everything is covered with no limits. It requires savings that can cover a $10k–$20k bad year.

Copay $2,500 if care is ongoing

Costs $3,765 more in premium. It pays that back in years with frequent mid-priced care, saving about $6,600 in a chronic-condition year. In quiet years and catastrophic ones it saves nothing.

VL $2,750 skip

Its caps leave a bad year with no ceiling. It has no out-of-network coverage and the highest out-of-pocket max of the three. It costs $2,025 more than CAT and covers less.

Will Congress remove the 400% cap?

Takeaway

I estimate about 10% in the lame-duck session and 25–35% during 2027. Democrats now control Congress, but any bill still needs Trump's signature.

~10%
Lame duck, through January 3. Republicans control it, and the December 11 funding deadline dominates.
25–35%
Law during 2027 that removes or raises the cap
56–44
Democratic Senate majority. That's short of 60, so a bill would likely go through reconciliation.
  • Where it stands: the House passed a three-year extension on January 8, 2026 (230–196). The Senate never voted, and no extension has become law for 2027.
  • The path in 2027: reconciliation needs only 51 votes and fits a tax credit; the 2021 expansion passed this way. It's slow, though, and Trump can still veto. Any deal might raise the cap rather than remove it.
  • Retroactivity: a 2027 law could apply back to January 1, as the 2021 law did, possibly with a special enrollment period. Neither is guaranteed.

These odds are my judgment from news coverage, not market odds.

Stay or switch: the decision rule

Takeaway

If 2027 income will be clearly over about $132,000, switch. Paying an extra $10k–$15k to wait on Kaiser only makes sense if a law is 66–93% likely.

Clearly under ~$132k

Stay on Kaiser

$8,022/yr and the best coverage. A new law would only make the subsidy bigger.

Near ~$132k

Stay, with a reduced advance subsidy

You keep the subsidy if you land under the line, and a law helps if you land over. A smaller advance limits what you'd owe back.

Clearly over ~$132k

Switch to CAT, or Copay $2,500

The chance of a law is too low to justify waiting.

At 30% odds, switching saves about $6,700 on average in a healthy year. Three more things push the answer toward switching:

  • The law may not help in 2027. It could start in 2028, or raise the cap to an income still below yours.
  • You may be able to move back. A mid-year special enrollment period could let you return to Covered CA, if AmeriBenefit allows mid-year cancellation.
  • Staying with the full advance subsidy has its own risk. If income lands over the cliff, you repay about $16,200 at tax time, with no cap.

Checklist before switching

Takeaway

Eight questions for the broker and one prescription check could each change the answer. Settle them before Covered CA open enrollment closes.

  • Eligibility: the family qualifies for the association plan. The rate sheet says "before employer contribution," which implies employee status.
  • The plan counts as Minimum Essential Coverage, so there's no CA mandate penalty
  • Final tier is Tier 2 at the 45–54 band, and the Family rate keys only off the subscriber's age
  • Mid-year cancellation is allowed, in case a subsidy law passes
  • Copays count toward the out-of-pocket max
  • If an in-network provider misses a precertification, the penalty falls on the provider, not on you
  • Whether a precertification penalty counts toward the out-of-pocket max. If it doesn't, a missed approval on a ~$60k hospital stay could cost ~$30,000 or the full bill.
  • How to confirm approval before a service: portal, or a call to Marpai (TPAX for VL)
  • Every current prescription is checked against the formulary. Brand-name drugs aren't covered on CAT, VL or Copay.
  • The family's doctors and nearest hospital are in-network: Cigna for CAT and Copay, PHCS for VL
  • APTC is set to $0 for any Covered CA months while expecting income over the cliff
  • 2027 enrollment deadlines are checked for both Covered CA and AmeriBenefit
