Export controls · AI compute · 13 Nov 2026 · updated 18 Nov

China's access to Nvidia compute: diversion, remote access and the coming controls

How Chinese labs could be training on Blackwells, how many chips may have gone missing through Megaspeed, how big a cluster that makes, and which US measures could cut off access.

In short: renting Blackwells in Southeast Asia is the likeliest training route, smuggling is about as large in volume, and Megaspeed's possible diversion is a frontier-class cluster for one lab but about 1% of China's compute.

The Commerce remote-access rule, issued November 16 and in force from November 19, and three bills headed for the defense bill target the two biggest channels. If they close them, IFP's numbers leave China about 40% short of its end-2027 projection.

An expert call says the move is already under way: Southeast Asia is "close to exhausted," and Chinese training is heading to Europe and to multi-gigawatt builds in Brazil. The rule as issued covers both: its remote-access control applies to data centers in any country, and Brazil is on its shipment list. Whether the 40% cut holds now turns on how many licenses BIS grants, not on geography.

New evidence: a November 12 forensic report finds six Chinese models released since April 2026 were started on Blackwell GPUs. It shows access, not location. Paul Triolo places only DeepSeek's Blackwells inside China (§1c). He also argues that closing the offshore channel speeds China's domestic stack (§7).

Digest figures are cited by digest date; outside research links to its source. Cluster benchmarks, chip specs and the compute arithmetic are general knowledge and my own math. Rule details come from the rule text (FR Doc. 2026-23529); bill details from press reports.

1 · Routes

How Chinese labs reach Blackwells

Frontier training needs one large, tightly networked cluster: NVL72 racks, high-speed fabric, power, and Nvidia firmware and support. That is easier to rent than to assemble from smuggled boxes, and renting is legal. "Export controls cover where a chip ships, not who uses it remotely" (Puklowski, Nov 8 digest).

Most likely for training

Renting in third countries

  • ~1M H100e of remote-access compute in Southeast Asia (Oct 20 digest).
  • Tencent's ~$7B Oracle lease (~100,000 chips), cancelled after Washington objected (Oct 21, Nov 8).
  • Aolani's planned ~36,000 B200s for ByteDance; 2,304 B200s rented in Norway via Nscale (Oct 12).
Gray zone

Nominal Southeast Asian clouds

  • Commerce stopped enforcing large-order licenses in May 2025; Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand imported ~$80B of AI chips this year (Nov 10).
  • Megaspeed: 17,544 GB300s in Nov 2025; a 50,000-unit gap in Nvidia's account to BIS (Nov 9).
Real, harder to use for training

Physical smuggling

  • B300 rack: $2.8M in China vs ~$600K elsewhere (Nov 6).
  • ~240,000 H100/H200/A100 via Century Litong, mostly pre-Blackwell (Nov 10).
  • Rebuilding smuggled racks into one cluster needs liquid cooling, networking and support (my reasoning).
1b · Expert call (received 13 Nov)

Training moves to Europe and Brazil

An unnamed expert in an expert-network-style Q&A (call date not shown; it treats the Oracle-Tencent deal as already halted). Claims are the expert's unless marked as checked. Transcript: trading/interviews/20261113-expert-call-china-overseas-compute-europe-brazil.md.

Southeast Asia

Strictest oversight, capacity "close to exhausted"

Matches Nvidia's 51% rule, chip tracking, Malaysia's permits and the Commerce rule's targets. US oversight of Europe is "second only to the US mainland."

Europe

Kimi and Tencent

Kimi seeks 8,000 B300s from a Chinese internet company's European cloud ("very likely"). Tencent eyes ~10,000-card sites in the UK and France, with partners buying B300s for its use. All for training; inference stays in China.

Brazil

Multi-gigawatt builds

ByteDance 1 GW first phase, ~1.1 GW second, over 2 GW total; Alibaba 700-800 MW first, 2-3 GW planned. Servers move in late 2026 to Q1 2027; service in H2 2027. "Most" non-US B300/GB300 shipments now go to Brazil.

My arithmetic: ~2.3 kW per Blackwell GPU all-in, so 1 GW ≈ 400-500K GPUs ≈ 1.0-1.25M H100e. These are upper bounds: "GW" may be phased facility capacity, and the halls may not be filled with Nvidia chips. IFP puts only ~0.7M H100e of end-2027 remote access outside Southeast Asia; the Brazil first phases alone would be 2.5-3x that.

Checks

ByteDance's Pecém, Ceará campus is reported at ~1 GW and ~$37.7B, developed by Casa dos Ventos and Omnia, operating from 2027, with talks on a similar second site (W.Media). Alibaba Cloud opened a two-site Brazil region Aug 27 (Mobile Time), no MW given. Kimi, Tencent-Europe and the Brazil shipment share are unconfirmed.

Caveats

Buying "directly from Nvidia" in a Chinese firm's name needs a license under the headquarters rule BIS restated May 31 (Greenberg Traurig); partner-owned chips rented remotely were taken not to, which is the route Tencent and Pecém fit. The November rule's preamble narrows that: if a Chinese firm was the primary (>50% of compute) user, BIS says the headquarters control already applied. "Chinese companies generally do not resort to smuggling" conflicts with Epoch and IFP, unless the expert means the majors themselves (Epoch: ~82% via intermediaries).

What happens to the Brazil builds under the November 16 rule

The rule doesn't stop construction; it decides what can go inside. Nearly every US AI chip for these halls ships after November 19 (server move-in late 2026 to Q1 2027, volume racking from Q2 2027; only shipments in transit keep the old terms for 30 days). Two licenses stand in the way, and they attach to different things, so a Brazilian owner of the chips gets past only the first (FR Doc. 2026-23529).

Shipping into Brazil · § 742.6

Brazil is one of the five listed countries. With ByteDance or Alibaba as consignee, review falls under the D:5 headquarters policy, a presumption of denial (the Nvidia page puts refusal at ~90-95%). The US-headquartered exemption helps only if a US cloud owns the chips.

Remote access · § 744.24

Whoever owns the chips, including Casa dos Ventos and Omnia at Pecém, needs a license once Chinese-parented tenants can reach more than 12M aggregate TPP (~200 B300s). The first phases are ~700-900K GPUs, thousands of times the threshold, summed across Chinese customers and linked sites. A US cloud hosting ByteDance there needs it too. Installed hardware has 90 days, to about Feb 17, 2027.

My expectation. The first phases finish roughly on schedule and fill with a mix: recommender and serving compute for ByteDance, general cloud for Alibaba, perhaps some Huawei racks, perhaps space leased to US clouds. Large Blackwell training clusters for Chinese labs there are unlikely without a policy reversal, and the second phases are the parts most likely to slow or shrink. That cuts the ~1.7-2.25M H100e upper bound above to a small fraction for frontier training. Tests: the license count by about Feb 17, 2027 and what gets racked from Q2 2027. The gigawatt figures rest on one unnamed expert; Pecém's ~1 GW matches press reports, Alibaba's 700-800 MW doesn't yet.

1c · Forensics (12-17 Nov)

Blackwell fingerprints in Chinese model weights

Will DePue (Unemployment Labs, Nov 12; code) reads the GPU that created a model's starting weights from its published weights. PyTorch's CUDA initializer spreads values across 2,048 threads per SM, so the same seed fills different positions on chips with different SM counts: 132 for H100/H800/H200, 148 for B200/B300, 152 for GB200/GB300. Untrained embedding rows keep those values and match at r > 0.9999 only under the right count.

Checks

The method agrees with all 24 models whose developers named the hardware, including Nvidia's Nemotron-3 Ultra (GB200). Stock PyTorch on a rented B300 and GB200 reproduced the matching rows; changing the seed by one matched nothing. A second researcher rebuilt the method (gpu-forensics-hf, Nov 16) and got the same layouts for four models. A commenter's figure of 65 matching models is unsourced.

Limits

The fingerprint shows the chip that created the starting weights, which supports, without proving, that it also did the training. It can't tell H800 from H100 or H200. A partitioned AMD MI300X also reads as 152 SMs, ruled out for V4-Flash. Nothing in the weights says where the chips were, who owned them, or whether a law was broken.

What it implies

The runs are small: DePue estimates ~12,000 Blackwells across five final runs (~30K H100e), a tenth of the Megaspeed fleet. Tencent's Hy4 and Alibaba's Qwen3.8 read as Hopper although both firms rent Blackwells. Meituan's LongCat-2.0 shows no Nvidia fingerprint, which fits its claim of ASIC training. My reading: once known, the fingerprint is easy to avoid, so it works best on models already released.

The legal question can turn on one line of a cap table. Renting Blackwell time in Thailand was legal for Moonshot. If an Alibaba subsidiary owns the 8,192 GPUs, shipping them needed a US license under the 2023 ultimate-parent rule; if Alibaba leases them from a non-Chinese operator, the chain is likely lawful. Triolo's legal reading predates the rule. BIS's preamble treats primary-user rentals as already covered, and the November 16 rule now licenses rentals above 12M TPP. A ChinaTalk commenter notes the rule measures access and the weights measure hardware, so a case needs both records joined.

Training compute (my arithmetic): 44,000 H100e × 0.99 PFLOPS × 40% utilization × 90 days ≈ 1.4 × 10²⁶ FLOP, about 40x DeepSeek V3 and several times GPT-4's estimated ~2 × 10²⁵. That is ~2.7x Llama 3's cluster and ~20x DeepSeek V3's, but about 4% of Stargate Abilene at full build and about 1% of China's total compute.

Against China's own largest site. Epoch (Cottier, Nov 6) puts Huawei's Horinger campus at ~81K H100e across four buildings (~286 MW), about 7% of Colossus 2's ~1.1M; only VNET Bayin Ulanqab and Huawei Wuhu are likely comparable. One month of Megaspeed GB300s is a bit over half of Horinger, so a diverted Blackwell cluster would rank among China's largest AI sites. Horinger should reach ~141K H100e around February 2028 with ~76,000 Ascend 950DTs; Colossus 2 should pass 1.8M by then.

A training cluster has to sit in one building on one fabric. 17,544 GB300s arriving in one month looks like one deployment; the 50,000 gap may be spread across sites and periods.

Speaker Johnson sees a "developing consensus"; Semafor reports the NDAA bills could pass by year-end. Nvidia is tightening too: a 51% non-Chinese capacity rule for cloud partners, chip tracking for Southeast Asian partners, and a halved list of approved Asian buyers. Malaysia now requires permits for US-origin accelerators.

Read against the rule text. US-headquartered buyers are exempt only from the five-country shipment licenses. The remote-access control has no US-company exemption, and no country list: it covers data centers "wherever located," Europe and Brazil included.

Look-through runs to the tenant's "ultimate parent." Data centers must check for indirect Chinese ownership. A "for an entity" clause covers non-Chinese middlemen training models for Chinese firms. Compute is summed across "linked data centers," and de minimis rules can't be used. The linked-center test turns on the network, not the country, so a Johor site linked to a US site would count the US capacity too.

What's left: data centers on US soil, which receive no export, reexport or in-country transfer for the license to attach to (McGuire: "Northern Virginia is going to become the center of China AI until we fix this," ChinaTalk); small rentals spread across unrelated providers; deals where no supplier has "knowledge"; and whatever licenses BIS grants under case-by-case review. BIS expects only ~100 more applications a year.

Nvidia's exposure is sized on the Nvidia revenue-at-risk page; the sales chain and Megaspeed on the sales-chain page.

6 · Impact

Effect on China's compute

ChinaTalk puts remote access at about two-fifths of China's AI compute (ChinaTalk). IFP's share is lower, ~22% at end-2026, but the absolute figures are close: two-fifths of ChinaTalk's ~2.8M H100e is ~1.1M, against IFP's 1.0M. IFP counts more domestic and smuggled compute.

Remote access matters most because it is the fastest-growing channel and holds the newest chips, including the large Blackwell blocks. Without it, Chinese labs are left with smaller, mixed clusters of smuggled and domestic chips, against a US lead IFP already puts at ~16x across top labs.

The geographic gap closed; the licensing gap opened. Before the text came out, the rule was reported as covering seven countries, with nothing on Europe or Brazil, where the expert says Chinese training is going. As issued, the remote-access control covers data centers anywhere, and Brazil is on the shipment list, so the "controls bite" bar holds on geography.

It can still be too low if BIS grants many case-by-case licenses, if training moves to US soil, or if enforcement against middlemen is weak. The tests are the license count in the 90 days to about February 17, 2027, and whether the Brazil halls fill with licensed Nvidia chips or with Huawei's.

7 · Responses

How China could mitigate

  1. Lock in capacity now. Reuters reports Chinese clouds reserving overseas Nvidia capacity (Quartz). The rule doesn't grandfather it: existing data centers get 90 days to obtain a license, and only shipments in transit get 30 days under the old terms.
  2. Move to uncovered countries. Under way, per the expert: Kimi and Tencent in Europe, ByteDance and Alibaba in Brazil, and ByteDance already renting in Norway. The rule closes this on paper, since its remote-access control covers every country. The open route is US soil.
  3. Partner-owned capacity. Tencent's partners buying B300s, Pecém's Brazilian developers, "Chinese capital, Singapore headquarters." BIS's preamble also says supplying a data center whose primary (>50%) user was a Chinese firm was already covered before the rule. The rule targets this through the ultimate-parent test, indirect-ownership checks and the "for an entity" clause. What survives is deals where no supplier has "knowledge."
  4. Keep smuggling at higher prices. Location verification mainly hits new chips.
  5. Domestic chips, capped by memory. CXMT's ~2-2.2M HBM stacks support ~250-400K Ascend 910C-class packages; SMIC could make 800K+ dies (Tom's Hardware). MATCH targets the DUV tools; CNAS names DUVi as a main evasion route. Kempkey: "Fabrication may not be the main constraint"; packaging and HBM may bind first. Triolo adds that CXMT starts limited HBM3E output with ~20% usable (SK Hynix ~70%), that Huawei pulled the Ascend 960DT forward to Q1 2027, and that Huali 7nm adds ~0.1-0.6M H100e in 2027. His end-2027 cases for Chinese-designed chips are ~3M (IFP baseline), 3.1-3.6M (with Huali), 4-5M (accelerated) and 6-8M (upside).
  6. Scale out with weaker chips. CloudMatrix 384, cheap power, the $300B National Data Group buildout. Epoch: B200/B300 are ~2.5x more energy-efficient than the 910C, so a watt buys ~40% of US compute; power isn't the limit, and the Ascend 950 is less efficient still, so growth comes from adding power. On these numbers brute force has reached ~7% of one US site.
  7. Efficiency and distillation. DeepSeek V3 on 2,048 H800s; can't fully replace raw compute at the frontier. CNAS counts adversarial distillation of US models as an evasion channel, one reason the lag may be ~6.5 months. DeepSeek released Ascend ports of its training stack on Sept 30. Triolo calls this the year's biggest change because it lowers CUDA lock-in; independent replication is still the test.
  8. Take the legal H200s. ~600,000 sit in Hong Kong because Beijing blocks them (Baker, Nov 9).
8 · Portfolio

Portfolio angles

Exposed

Southeast Asian neoclouds and data-center operators (Johor), US clouds renting to Chinese firms, Nvidia's Southeast Asia billings.

Beneficiaries

China's domestic chain (Huawei suppliers, SMIC, Cambricon, CXMT) and Chinese power builders. ASML and Tokyo Electron lose DUV sales if MATCH passes.

Now covered

European colocation hosts (UK, France, Nordics) and Brazilian builders and power developers (Casa dos Ventos, Pátria's Omnia) become license applicants. Without licenses, the Brazil halls fill with Huawei chips or sit partly empty.

Watch

How many remote-access licenses BIS grants by about Feb 17, 2027 (~100 more applications a year expected), any move to reach US-soil data centers (RASA), Brazil handovers from Q2 2027, NDAA conference, Nvidia's Nov 17 Southeast Asia billings, BIS or DOJ chip counts, whether Beijing releases the H200s, Megaspeed's Entity List listing, whether officials cite the model-weight evidence, CXMT HBM3E yields and Huali 7nm volume, BIS funding or STRIDE, Huawei's usable output vs. Epoch's ~880K, Horinger's fourth building (Dec 2026) and 950 expansion (~Feb 2028).
