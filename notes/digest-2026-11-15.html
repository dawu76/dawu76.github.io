Volatility: The Vanna Rally and What's Left of It

Monday's 23% VIX collapse forced dealers to buy back hedges, the vanna rally. By Friday implied volatility was below realized, the variance premium was negative at the front, and Sharpe Two told readers to stop selling options.

  • The vanna rallyVIX -23% Monday, 15th-largest since 1990; SPX 7,563 under the 7,590 pivot

    How falling volatility lifted stocks (SpotGamma, "The market's rebound fuel runs low"). The VIX fell nearly 23% on Monday, the 15th-largest one-day percentage drop since 1990, and by Wednesday the term structure was nearly back to pre-selloff levels. Dealers who had sold puts hedged by selling stock; as implied volatility collapsed, the vanna effect made them buy that stock back, "jetpack fuel" for the rally.

    By Friday implied volatility was below realized, "most of that fuel looked spent," and SPX closed at 7,563, below SpotGamma's 7,590 risk pivot, with 7,700 as major resistance and 7,400 as major support. SPX traded in a range of 1% or more in every session; one-month realized volatility is near 20%.

    • Why it matters a rally powered by hedges unwinding stops when there are no hedges left to unwind. SpotGamma expects the next move to come from the events, not from volatility falling further, and sees room for a rally into Thanksgiving if they pass, as after last November's expiration.
  • Poorly paid to sell insuranceVIX 19.3; front variance premium -8; vol-of-vol ~205

    Poorly paid to sell insurance (Sharpe Two, "Forward Note - 20261115"). The VIX fell to 17.5 midweek and closed at 19.3; every SPY session since November 5's 32.5 realized volatility printed between about 18.5 and 21, against an 11.9 median since mid-August. Thirty-day realized volatility is about 15 and forecast toward 18.5-19 over the next month.

  • Nvidia options turnedPut IV 63% Tuesday; $1.07B delta Thursday; $50M Mar-27 call spread

    Nvidia's options turned (SpotGamma; Sharpe Two). Nvidia's 30-day 25-delta put volatility reached 63% Tuesday, its second-highest reading of the past year. Thursday's low of $148.80 marked the turn: Nvidia led all stocks with $1.07B of delta, mostly from closing December and January puts, and $475M of call premium.

    Friday brought a $50M March 2027 $175/$225 call spread on 43,000 contracts, bought with front-month volatility near 60 and an IV rank of 95. Sharpe Two sees Nvidia's volatility, still near 60, falling to the high 40s after earnings and taking the VIX with it.

  • What a crash put is worth3% a year costs ~1.5% of CAGR; at 1% it about breaks even

    Puts priced as insurance, without volatility (Kris Abdelmessih, Moontower #336). Abdelmessih's exercise: hold SPX and buy a one-year 15% out-of-the-money put each January for 3%. Over 25 years it pays off meaningfully in 2008 (about 7x), perhaps a little in 2002 and 2022, and loses its full cost the other 22 years, which costs about 1.8% a year of arithmetic return.

    Because it removes about three-quarters of 2008's variance, volatility drops from about 16% to 14%, and the CAGR falls only about 1.5%, from roughly 8.7% to 7.2%. At a 1% premium the put about breaks even.

    He links it to Jeff Yass's WSJ op-ed that there is no such thing as a bubble, only "uncertainty resolving," and his own line that the resolution of uncertainty is usually bearish.

    • Why it matters with front-month variance premium negative (Sharpe Two), protection is about as cheap relative to realized moves as it gets; Abdelmessih's math says what a fair long-run price for a crash put looks like.

Nvidia Without Its Kingmaker: The Financing Web

Nvidia carries $530B of off-balance-sheet commitments and a $99B equity book, much of it deals Huang priced himself. Without him the neoclouds lose a patron, and the frontier labs have already moved most new compute elsewhere.

  • AI's central bank, no chairman$530B from $184B; $36B floors; $140B in two months; Kress halved a $250B backstop

    The central bank of AI loses its chairman (Michael Parekh, AI-RTZ #1271, "The World Without the Kingmaker"). Citing SemiAnalysis, Parekh lists $530B of gross off-balance-sheet guarantees at the end of the fiscal second quarter, up from $184B a quarter earlier, including $36B of take-or-pay floors under neocloud capacity, alongside a $99B equity book. The FT counts more than 120 investments this year and up to $105B of credit support behind OpenAI's Ohio campus; The Information tallies $140B of deals and guarantees in two months.

    Kress pushed back on a $250B OpenAI backstop that was cut in two.

    "For a market that priced in the 'Jensen put', that is a tightening of AI credit, even if no one calls it that."
    • Vs. prior digests the $530B, $184B, $36B and $105B figures match the 11-14 digest (market-newsletter-digest-2026-11-14 §2). The equity book is $99B here, $98.9B from Noble on 11-14 and $94B at the end of July from Evan Armstrong below; the gap is rounding and dating.
  • 58% of operating cash to stakesDebt and leases $214B to $422B; circular deals >50% of AI investment

    Where the cash went (Reader: Michael Cembalest, J.P. Morgan, "The Icarus Files: What's next for Nvidia and AI"). Over the last three quarters Nvidia spent 58% of its operating cash flow on equity investments and M&A. From Q1 to Q2 its off-balance-sheet long-term debt and lease liabilities rose from $214B to $422B, and $530B with land, power and shell guarantees.

    The BIS wrote a special report on circular AI deals in October; circular and AI-to-AI deals were more than 50% of investment in AI firms from 2021 to 2025. Yields on AI infrastructure and SPV debt, high-yield and investment-grade, have risen sharply this year and started to weigh on high yield overall.

    • Different measureParekh's $184B to $530B is the gross guarantee total; Cembalest's $214B to $422B is debt and lease liabilities alone, and both reach $530B with land, power and shell. See spv-history-and-gpu-project-finance.
  • Deals on a handshake$94B of stakes from $35B; Groq $20B; Hugging Face 80x revenue

    The deals Huang closed himself (Evan Armstrong, The Leverage, "Nvidia Learns the Danger of Broken Promises"). Nvidia held $94B of equity stakes at the end of July, up from $35B six months earlier, in 9 model makers, 7 clouds and a list of applications, while 3 customers made up 44% of sales in the six months to July and every big buyer is building its own chip. Armstrong's list: Groq got $20B three weeks after asking to buy 100,000 GPUs, three months after a $6.9B valuation; the $100B OpenAI pact was negotiated one-on-one, and Microsoft learned of it the day before; Hugging Face went for $12.9B, more than 80x revenue; $5B went to Safe Superintelligence and $7B to license Poolside.

    "Tens of billions of dollars in liquidity and capital just came to a screeching halt."
    • Why it matters if the deal flow depended on one person setting prices on a call, a cautious successor removes it even if no contract changes.
  • Neoclouds lose their patron15 deals, $24B of rounds; CoreWeave debt 15%+; AI infra index -36%

    Who loses (Parekh; Cembalest; Rich Excell, Stay Vigilant, "What would happen if the sun went out?"). Per the FT, Nvidia has done at least 15 neocloud deals this year, in rounds raising $24B, plus $20B of leases it plans to hand to others. One neocloud executive told The Information it is now considering Google and AMD chips, which it had held back on "for fear of offending him."

    Cembalest names CoreWeave, Nebius, Applied Digital and IREN as exposed. Excell likens neoclouds to subprime lenders before 2008, "the weakest of the herd," with CoreWeave debt yielding 15%+ and 20% of its float short; his AI data-center and electrical index is down 36% this year.

    • Counterpoint (Parekh): signed deals look safe, Hugging Face is owned rather than rented, and any Nvidia CEO who understands the "frenemies" math keeps funding open models because they create customers beyond the labs building their own chips.
  • 39% of future frontier-lab computeFrom 80%+; AMD onboarding 6-12 months to 3 days

    Supply chain and share (Parekh; Cembalest). SemiAnalysis estimates Nvidia pays SK Hynix about $3.0-3.3 per gigabit for 2027 HBM4, against $3.7-4.1 for other big buyers, and its supply commitments jumped from $119B to $279B in one quarter, mostly memory; it expects more than 70% of AI capacity to be non-Nvidia by 2028, which Parekh thinks is too high. Cembalest's chart has Nvidia at 39% of future frontier-lab compute agreements, against an 80%+ share in 2023-2025.

Valuing Nvidia: Targets Cut, a Cheap PEG and a "Jensen Premium"

Nvidia fell about 13% for the week as AMD and Broadcom rose about 18%. Analysts cut targets to $186-300, all still above the close, and AlphaDoc's model says the market prices only about 10% growth for a decade at the lows.

  • -13% while semis rose 8%Capitulation signal at $149; $135-140 the support to watch

    The price and the chart (TrendSpider; J.L. Bernstein, Pivot & Flow, "Hackers turned 700 freezers into ovens."; Excell). Nvidia fell nearly 13% for the week (Bernstein: 12.75%, its sixth straight weekly loss) while semis rose about 8%; AMD and Broadcom each gained about 18% (Bernstein) or AMD 19% (TrendSpider). The flush to $149 set off Nvidia's first bullish capitulation signal since the April 2025 lows.

    Excell has it at $162.85, down 20% for the month and from about $243 in early October, and calls $135-140, where the 2025 open and the trend line from 2024 meet, "extremely critical support."

    • ConflictExcell's $243 early-October level against the $239.24 October 6 record close in market-newsletter-digest-2026-11-14 §2; his figure may be an intraday high.
  • PEG of 0.2x8.5% of the S&P in May; 75% gross, 66% operating margins; net cash

    Cheap on the numbers (Reader: Cembalest). Nvidia rose from 1% of the S&P in December 2022 to 8.5% in May 2026, faster than any of the ten companies that have been the index's largest since 1960. It earned 83% of AI chip revenue in 2023-2025 at 75% gross and 66% operating margins, trades at a PEG of 0.2x, the lowest among large AI stocks by a wide margin, and has net debt below zero.

    Cembalest thinks it "looks interesting for long term holders" at 32% below its high, though competition is "just as notable a risk" as the management change. Retail treated the 13% drop as a buying opportunity, as it did in February.

    • Adam Tooze (Chartbook), quoting the FT: the $4.5T company is nearly 7% of the S&P, and Huang "was a cheerleader when tech investors lost their nerve," most recently during the SaaSpocalypse.
  • Targets cut to $186-300Baird $500 to $200; Evercore $413 to $186; UBS raised to $300

    Price targets cut (Cembalest). Consensus had implied about 40% upside before the scandal. Since July 1, Baird cut from $500 to $200 (-60%), Evercore ISI from $413 to $186 (-55%), Bernstein from $315 to $200, Citi $300 to $225, JP Morgan $280 to $215, Goldman $285 to $220, Morgan Stanley $288 to $267 and Wells Fargo $315 to $300; UBS raised from $280 to $300.

    • Why it matters the cuts cluster near $200-225, about 23-38% above Friday's close of $162.85, so the Street has cut its targets without turning bearish.
  • The Jensen premium$173.73 implied 15.5% growth for 10 years; 10 years instead of 15 costs ~$45

    What the price implies (Reader: AlphaDoc, "NVIDIA (NVDA): The Jensen Premium"). Using $171.7B of R&D-adjusted NOPAT, a 10% cost of capital and 30% returns on new investment, the November 11 close of $173.73 implied 15.5% annual NOPAT growth for a 10-year competitive advantage period, 11.8% for 15 years or 10% for 20. At the Thursday low the market priced about 10% growth for 10 years, "fairly low expectations."

Huang had more than 60 direct reports. On succession, Kress is the right interim choice, Ian Buck is continuity, Rene Haas the outside executive, and Jonathan Ross, who built Google's first TPU and Groq, the dark horse who could disrupt Nvidia from inside; a DOJ review and a Delaware suit over the Groq deal complicate Ross.
    • Why it matters AlphaDoc's point is that succession shows up in how long Nvidia earns excess returns, not in next quarter's earnings, so Tuesday's report can be strong and still not settle the question.

Anthropic's IPO: The Commitments and the Economics

  • Mobile, not fiber3G operators paid, Apple and Google won; variable costs act fixed

    Mobile, not fiber (Stephen Clapham, Behind the Balance Sheet, "Who Gets Rich from AI?"). With Benedict Evans, Clapham rejects the dot-com fiber analogy: fiber was laid ahead of demand and could be upgraded cheaply, while AI demand runs ahead of supply and every increment needs more chips, power and cooling. The better parallel is 3G: operators were right about the technology but paid for infrastructure whose value went to Apple, Google, Meta and Uber, most of which weren't investable yet.

    Evans's point on the accounts: compute cost of sales has been falling while training, booked in R&D, rises with revenue, so "variable costs look like they have become fixed and fixed costs look like they have become variable."

How Far Could Stocks Fall?

Furman's baseline for a burst AI bubble is a 25% fall in the broad market, about $19T of household stock, while options price an unusually small chance of a crash. Yang's wealth-effect math puts the pain point much closer, near 7,000-7,200.

  • A 25% baselineForward P/E 18.3, CAPE 40; scenarios from -11% to -50%

    The baseline is 25% (Reader: Jason Furman, "How Far Could Stocks Fall?"). Furman keeps his money in Vanguard's VT and doesn't expect a crash, but sizes one if the AI boom is a bubble. A 50% fall in Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom and Oracle alone takes 11% off the market.

    The forward P/E of 18.3 (77th percentile since 1979) falling to its 15.0 average is an 18% decline, 34% with 20% lower earnings; the CAPE of 40 (99th percentile since 1881) returning to 23.6 is 41%; reversing the market's excess over nominal GDP since 2019 is 34%. His baseline is 25%, a bad case 35% and a severe, unlikely case 50%.

    Even 25% wipes out about $19T of household stock.

Cisco traded at 213 times trailing earnings against Nvidia's 21, and 102 times forward against 13. H100 rental prices are up more than 50% since last December.
